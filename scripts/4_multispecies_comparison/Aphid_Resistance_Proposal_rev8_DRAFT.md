## Abstract

*Aphis gossypii* (cotton-melon aphid) causes direct yield losses and transmits destructive viruses in cucurbit crops. Warming temperatures intensify aphid pressure while insecticide resistance undermines chemical control, making host plant resistance essential. Yet the genetics of resistance remain poorly characterized: in melon, all QTL data derive from a single cross; in watermelon and squash, no resistance loci have been mapped. This project will screen approximately 510 accessions across three crops — the *Cucurbita pepo* Newe Ya'ar collection, a multi-species *Citrullus* collection and BC₃F₄ mapping population, and the Melo180 *Cucumis melo* diversity panel — using a standardized whole-plant bioassay. Heritability will be estimated in each crop as a decision point for mapping. Genome-wide association mapping (GWAS) will be conducted in melon and squash using existing SNP datasets. Bi-parental QTL mapping via BSA-Seq will be performed in watermelon and melon populations. Introgression of resistance into commercial backgrounds will begin within the project period. Deliverables include characterized germplasm, QTL maps, molecular markers for marker-assisted selection, and initial breeding lines.

## Introduction

Melon (*Cucumis melo*), watermelon (*Citrullus lanatus*), and squash (*Cucurbita pepo*) are among the highest-value vegetable crops in Israel. All three face mounting pressure from *Aphis gossypii* Glover (cotton-melon aphid), a phloem-feeding pest whose economic impact extends beyond direct feeding damage to the efficient non-persistent transmission of Cucumber mosaic virus, Watermelon mosaic virus, and Zucchini yellow mosaic virus. Rising temperatures are expanding aphid population cycles, while growing insecticide resistance in *A. gossypii* populations limits the efficacy of chemical control (Carletto et al. 2010). Host plant resistance offers the most sustainable management strategy.

In melon, resistance to *A. gossypii* is mediated primarily by *Vat*, a CC-NBS-LRR gene on chromosome 5 that confers both antibiosis against aphid colonization and resistance to virus transmission (Dogimont et al. 2014). The *Vat* locus explains 61–75% of resistance variance depending on aphid clone, with additional minor QTLs on chromosomes IV and IX contributing 4–9% each (Boissot et al. 2010). However, a critical limitation constrains the current knowledge: every QTL effect size estimate derives from a single genetic cross (Védrantais × PI 161375), phenotyped with French aphid clones. No cross-background validation has been performed, and the behaviour of resistance in the commercially dominant inodorus market type is unknown. Recent genomic surveys have catalogued hundreds of *Vat* allelic variants (Belinchon-Moreno et al. 2025), yet none of this work included aphid phenotyping. Of 177 Melo180 accessions cross-referenced against published screening records, 171 have no documented *A. gossypii* resistance phenotype (Appendix 1). For watermelon, germplasm screens have identified putatively resistant accessions in *Citrullus colocynthis* and other wild relatives, but no resistance QTLs, genes, or molecular markers have been characterized in any *Citrullus* species. For squash, the situation is identical: no genetic analysis of *A. gossypii* resistance has been conducted in *Cucurbita pepo* or any related species.

This project addresses these gaps using germplasm resources uniquely available at the Volcani Institute and Newe Ya'ar Research Center. The Newe Ya'ar *C. pepo* collection (~200 accessions, GBS-genotyped with 45,000 SNPs), a multi-species *Citrullus* collection and BC₃F₄ *C. colocynthis* × *C. lanatus* mapping population, and the Melo180 melon diversity panel (180 accessions, 23,931 SNPs; Gur et al. 2017) are all available for screening and genetic analysis. Preliminary screening of a 25-accession melon subset has already identified resistant and susceptible lines across multiple botanical groups (Table 1; Figure 1). Together, these resources enable a systematic program of resistance screening, heritability estimation, genetic mapping, and breeding application across three cucurbit crops within a single coordinated project.

## Preliminary Results

We performed a preliminary screening of MelonCore25, a representative subset of the Melo180 collection (Gur et al., 2017; Table 1; Figure 1). Three patterns emerge. First, known *Vat* carriers PI 161375 and PI 414723 scored as resistant, validating the bioassay. Second, several accessions from botanical groups not previously tested for aphid resistance (including flexuosus and agrestis types) showed intermediate or resistant phenotypes, suggesting resistance sources beyond the known *Vat* donors. Third, inodorus accessions showed variable responses, consistent with the unresolved question of *Vat* function in this market type.

## Objectives

This project aims to screen, genetically map, and initiate breeding application of *Aphis gossypii* resistance in three cucurbit crops. The central hypothesis is that heritable resistance exists in available germplasm of each crop, that resistance loci can be mapped using existing genomic resources combined with systematic phenotyping, and that introgression into commercial backgrounds can begin within the project period. Objectives are organized by crop, reflecting the distinct germplasm, starting points, and expected endpoints for each species.

**Objective 1 — *Cucurbita pepo* (squash): Screening, heritability estimation, and population development.** Screen the Newe Ya'ar *C. pepo* collection (~200 accessions) for *A. gossypii* resistance, estimate heritability and mode of inheritance, conduct GWAS using 45,000 GBS-derived SNPs, develop F₂ mapping populations from resistant × susceptible crosses, and initiate introgression of resistance into a commercial cocozelle background.

**Objective 2 — *Citrullus* (watermelon): Mapping resistance from wild relatives and collection screening.** Screen the BC₃F₄ *C. colocynthis* × *C. lanatus* mapping population and a multi-species *Citrullus* collection (~30 accessions spanning *C. colocynthis*, *C. amarus*, *C. mucospermus*, and *C. lanatus*) for *A. gossypii* resistance. Map resistance QTLs in the BC₃F₄ via BSA-Seq. Initiate new interspecific crosses based on collection screening results, and begin introgression of resistance into commercial watermelon backgrounds.

**Objective 3 — *Cucumis melo* (melon): Multi-background GWAS, QTL validation, and introgression.** Screen the Melo180 diversity panel for *A. gossypii* resistance, conduct GWAS to identify resistance loci beyond the known *Vat* region on chromosome 5, validate GWAS hits via BSA-Seq in existing F₂ populations, develop molecular markers for marker-assisted selection, and initiate backcrossing of non-*Vat* resistant accessions into Galia-type elite breeding lines.

## Research Plan

### Phenotyping platform and experimental design

All three crops will be screened using a standardized whole-plant choice bioassay developed and validated in Aviv Dombrovsky's laboratory at the Volcani Institute (Figure 2). Seedlings at the two-to-three true leaf stage are placed in a greenhouse screening chamber and exposed to *A. gossypii* from a laboratory colony maintained on cucurbit hosts. Plants are scored on a categorical damage scale at [**X days**] post-infestation. In diversity panel screens, each accession receives a single score per plot (9 plants). In segregating populations, individual plants are scored separately to capture within-family variation.

Screening follows a replicated block design optimized for the chamber capacity (25 trays; tray dimensions: 19 columns × 9 rows). Each accession occupies a 2-column checkered plot (9 plants, ~50% pot occupancy) with no separators between plots. Column 19 of each tray is reserved for a within-tray resistant or susceptible control (5 plants). Each species is screened as a single cycle (one block), and three blocks are completed per species, providing the replication necessary for robust heritability estimation.

Screening will run at two sites — Beit Dagan (Volcani Institute) and Newe Ya'ar Research Center — under the guidance of Aviv Dombrovsky, enabling parallel block execution. Each block requires approximately one month from planting to scoring. Species will be screened in staggered sequence beginning with *C. pepo*, followed by *Citrullus*, then *C. melo*. With two-site parallelism, the complete first-round screening of all three crops (510 accessions total; 58 tray-runs) is expected within 5–6 months.

The chamber loads per species are: squash (*Cucurbita pepo*): 192 accessions (188 collection + ~4 commercial controls), 22 trays per block; watermelon (*Citrullus* spp.): 134 accessions (100 BC₃F₄ lines + 30 collection accessions representing *C. colocynthis*, *C. amarus*, *C. mucospermus*, and *C. lanatus* + ~4 commercial *C. lanatus* controls), 15 trays per block; melon (*Cucumis melo*): 184 accessions (180 Melo180 panel + ~4 commercial controls), 21 trays per block. Commercial cultivars of each species are included as marketplace checks to anchor the resistance scale in a breeding-relevant context. [**Placeholder: external advisors from the Israeli seed industry will be consulted on cultivar selection.**]

**Heritability estimation and decision framework.** Broad-sense heritability (H²) will be estimated for each crop from a two-way ANOVA (genotype, block) on the replicated screening data, incorporating the between-site variance component. H² serves as an explicit go/no-go decision point: low heritability would indicate that environmental noise overwhelms genetic signal under the bioassay conditions, and mapping and crossing activities will not be initiated for that crop. The screening dataset itself remains a deliverable. For crops with adequate H², the shape of the phenotypic distribution will inform the mapping strategy: a bimodal distribution suggests major-gene resistance amenable to BSA-Seq with small bulk sizes, whereas a continuous distribution indicates polygenic control requiring larger mapping populations.

**Tail validation.** Following the initial full screen, the extreme tails of each diversity panel (approximately 20–30 accessions per crop representing the most resistant and most susceptible lines) will be re-screened in the same 3-block design in a second year. This confirmation step distinguishes genuine genetic resistance from environmental noise, produces high-confidence phenotypic classifications for GWAS input, and provides a cross-year component to heritability estimation. Tail validation will run in parallel with bi-parental population screening, exploiting the low chamber load (~4–5 trays per species per block). For the watermelon BC₃F₄ mapping population, all 100 lines are phenotyped in all 3 blocks during the first screen, since individual-line phenotypes are required for QTL mapping; tail validation applies to the *Citrullus* diversity collection and the melon and squash panels.

### Genotyping and mapping strategy

Two complementary approaches will be used for genetic mapping across the three crops.

**Genome-wide association study (GWAS)** will be applied to the melon (Melo180) and squash (Newe Ya'ar collection) diversity panels using existing GBS-derived SNP datasets. Association analysis will use a mixed linear model (MLM) incorporating population structure (principal components) and kinship to control for false positives. For melon, the existing 23,931-SNP dataset (Gur et al. 2017) provides sufficient resolution given LD decay at ~100 Kb. For squash, a 45,000-SNP GBS-derived dataset is available for the Newe Ya'ar collection.

**Bulked segregant analysis (BSA-Seq)** will be used for QTL mapping in bi-parental populations. Following phenotyping, DNA from the resistant and susceptible tails of each segregating population will be pooled into bulks and subjected to whole-genome sequencing. QTL intervals will be identified by sliding-window analysis of allele frequency differences (Δ-SNP index) between bulks, anchored to the respective reference genomes. BSA-Seq is preferred over individual-plant GBS for bi-parental mapping because it reduces per-sample sequencing costs while providing genome-wide coverage sufficient for QTL interval identification. PCR-based markers (KASP or CAPS) will subsequently be developed within QTL intervals for validation and breeding application.

Candidate gene analysis within mapped QTL intervals will leverage the melon pan-genome (Oren et al. 2022) and the published watermelon T2T super-pangenome (Zhang et al. 2024) to resolve structural variation and allelic diversity beyond what single-reference mapping can detect.


### *Cucurbita pepo* (squash): Screening, mapping, and population development

**Germplasm.** The Newe Ya'ar *Cucurbita pepo* collection comprises approximately 200 accessions spanning both *C. pepo* subsp. *pepo* and subsp. *ovifera*, including pumpkin, vegetable marrow, zucchini, cocozelle, acorn, crookneck, and ornamental types (Paris, 2001). No *A. gossypii* resistance QTLs, genes, or molecular markers have been reported in any *Cucurbita* species. This objective represents the first genetic investigation of aphid resistance in squash.

**Year 1 — Full screening and heritability.** The complete collection (192 accessions including commercial controls) will be screened in 3 blocks. Resistance scores will be analyzed to estimate H² and characterize the phenotypic distribution. If H² is adequate, crosses between resistant and susceptible parents will be initiated in late Year 1 to develop an F₂ mapping population (~200 plants). In parallel, a cross between the most resistant accession and a Newe Ya'ar-bred cocozelle cultivar will be initiated to begin introgression into commercially relevant germplasm. If H² is low, the screening dataset remains a deliverable but mapping and crossing activities will not proceed.

**Year 2 — Tail validation, GWAS, and population advancement.** The collection tails (~30 accessions) will be re-screened for confirmation. GWAS will be conducted using validated phenotype data and the 45,000-SNP dataset. F₁ plants from Year 1 crosses will be selfed to produce F₂ seed. The cocozelle introgression cross will be advanced to BC₁.

**Year 3 — F₂ phenotyping and BSA-Seq.** The F₂ population (~200 plants) will be phenotyped with categorical per-plant scoring. DNA from the resistant and susceptible tails will be bulked for BSA-Seq.

**Deliverables:** Validated resistant and susceptible accessions with H² and mode-of-inheritance estimates; first GWAS for *A. gossypii* resistance in *Cucurbita*; F₂ population phenotyped and genotyped by BSA-Seq; BC₁ introgression lines (resistant × cocozelle) advancing. QTL fine-mapping and marker development will follow in a subsequent project.


### *Citrullus* (watermelon): Mapping resistance from wild relatives and collection screening

**Germplasm.** Two resources will be screened. The BC₃F₄ *Citrullus colocynthis* × *C. lanatus* mapping population consists of approximately 100 lines backcrossed three times to cultivated watermelon and selfed through four generations, retaining introgressed segments from the wild parent. *De novo* interspecific crosses between *C. colocynthis* and *C. lanatus* are impractical due to severe interspecific sterility, making this existing backcross population the primary mapping resource for colocynthis-derived resistance. Literature documents *A. gossypii* resistance in *C. colocynthis* germplasm, providing the biological rationale.

A *Citrullus* diversity collection of approximately 30 accessions held at Newe Ya'ar, representing *C. colocynthis*, *C. amarus*, *C. mucospermus*, and *C. lanatus*, will be screened to identify additional resistance sources and parents for new bi-parental populations. Commercial *C. lanatus* cultivars will serve as marketplace checks. Resistance in cultivated watermelon (*C. lanatus*) is not expected; sources are anticipated from the wild and semi-wild species.

**Year 1 — Full screening and heritability.** Both populations will be screened in 3 blocks. All 100 BC₃F₄ lines will be phenotyped in every block for QTL mapping. H² will be estimated for the diversity collection.

**Year 2 — BSA-Seq mapping, tail validation, and new crosses.** BC₃F₄ phenotypic data will be used for BSA-Seq: resistant and susceptible tails bulked and sequenced, with QTL intervals identified against the watermelon reference genome. The watermelon T2T super-pangenome (Zhang et al. 2024) will support candidate resolution, including analysis of the *Vat*-syntenic region as a targeted candidate. The diversity collection tails will be re-screened. New interspecific crosses will be initiated based on collection results, following the same backcross strategy used to create the existing BC₃F₄. Introgression of confirmed resistant accessions into commercial watermelon backgrounds will begin immediately. The BC₃F₄ will continue to be selfed toward fixation.

**Year 3 — QTL analysis, marker development, and population advancement.** QTL mapping will be completed and markers designed for the most significant intervals. New bi-parental populations will be advanced. Introgression lines will be evaluated.

**Deliverables:** QTL map for *A. gossypii* resistance in the BC₃F₄, the first in any *Citrullus* species; molecular markers for validated QTL intervals; characterized *Citrullus* diversity collection across four species; new interspecific populations advancing; initial introgression lines in commercial watermelon background.


### *Cucumis melo* (melon): Multi-background GWAS, QTL validation, and introgression

**Germplasm.** The Melo180 panel (180 inbred accessions; Gur et al. 2017) represents the full genetic breadth of *Cucumis melo* including cantalupensis, inodorus, flexuosus, agrestis, momordica, and wild relatives, genotyped with 23,931 SNPs. Of 177 accessions cross-referenced against published records, 171 have no documented *A. gossypii* resistance phenotype (Appendix 1). Three accessions are confirmed *Vat* carriers. Preliminary screening of a 25-accession subset identified resistant and susceptible lines across multiple botanical groups (Table 1; Figure 1). Existing F₂ populations from prior crosses are available for bi-parental validation. Melon is the most advanced crop in this project: the primary discovery target is non-*Vat* resistance loci, with the chromosome 5 *Vat* region serving as a positive GWAS control.

**Year 1 — Full screening and heritability.** The complete panel (184 accessions including commercial melon cultivars as controls) will be screened in 3 blocks. H² will be estimated and the phenotypic distribution characterized across botanical groups. Known *Vat* carriers serve as internal bioassay validation.

**Year 2 — Tail validation, GWAS, F₂ phenotyping, and introgression.** Melo180 tails (~30 accessions) will be re-screened. GWAS will be conducted with validated phenotypes, with the *Vat* locus on chromosome 5 expected as the strongest signal; the primary analytical focus will be non-chromosome-5 associations indicating novel resistance loci. Existing F₂ populations will be phenotyped and tails bulked for BSA-Seq. Backcrossing of non-*Vat* resistant accessions into Galia-type elite lines will be initiated.

**Year 3 — QTL validation, marker development, and backcross advancement.** BSA-Seq results will validate and fine-map GWAS-identified intervals. Molecular markers (KASP or CAPS) will be designed for confirmed QTLs, prioritizing non-*Vat* loci. Markers will be validated in an independent Melo180 subset. Backcross lines (BC₂–BC₃) will be advanced using marker-assisted selection where markers are available.

**Deliverables:** Complete resistance phenotype dataset for Melo180 with H² estimates; GWAS results including non-*Vat* loci; BSA-Seq QTL map from F₂ populations; validated molecular markers for MAS; introgression lines carrying non-*Vat* resistance in Galia-type background (BC₂–BC₃).


### Marker development

Molecular markers will be designed flanking confirmed QTL intervals identified by GWAS and/or BSA-Seq. Marker format (KASP or CAPS) will be selected based on locus characteristics. Markers will be validated in independent germplasm to confirm transferability across backgrounds. Development priority reflects the pace of QTL discovery: melon markers are expected first (Year 2–3), watermelon markers follow from BC₃F₄ mapping (Year 3), and squash markers are contingent on F₂ BSA-Seq results and more likely to be developed in a follow-up project. All validated markers and protocols will be made available to Israeli breeding programs.

## Expected Outcomes

This project will deliver three categories of output: breeding tools, genetic resources, and scientific knowledge.

On the breeding tools side, the project will produce validated molecular markers for marker-assisted selection of *A. gossypii* resistance in melon and watermelon, with protocols ready for deployment in backcrossing programs. Introgression lines carrying resistance in elite melon (Galia-type) and commercial watermelon backgrounds will be advancing by project end. In squash, BC₁ introgression lines in a cocozelle background will provide the foundation for marker-assisted breeding once QTLs are validated in a follow-up project.

On the genetic resources side, the project will characterize resistance across approximately 510 accessions spanning three crops, identifying confirmed resistant sources in each. These accessions, with their phenotypic and genotypic profiles, will constitute a publicly documented resource for cucurbit resistance breeding.

On the scientific side, the project will produce the first GWAS for *A. gossypii* resistance in melon across a panel representing all major market types, the first QTL map for resistance in any *Citrullus* population, and the first genome-wide association data for resistance in *Cucurbita*. Heritability estimates and mode-of-inheritance characterization for each crop will provide the foundational parameters needed for designing future breeding strategies.
